# Pitch deck content — 12 slides

The live deck is `/presentation` (`src/components/presentation/slides.tsx`); this is the same content for a PDF or printed hand-out. Every slide carries a stage badge; nothing claims authorisation.

## 1 · The problem — Stage 0
Irish people can’t buy, rents punish, and homes sit empty. 81,373 vacant homes plus 19,137 derelict (GeoDirectory Residential Buildings Report, Q2 2026). New-tenancy rents up 9.1% in a year to €1,839 a month (RTB Rent Index, Q1 2026). National median home price €400,000, up 5.1% (CSO RPPI, 12 months to Aug 2026). Property wealth is built by those who can already buy. Sources and periods: `docs/investor/STATISTICS_UPDATE_2026.md`.

## 2 · The mission — Stage 0
Meithera lets ordinary people fund the refurbishment of vacant homes from €100, through an EU crowdfunding licence (ECSPR), with every euro, holder and payout recorded on Raya Chain and visible to the supervisor. Over time, tenants can buy into the home they live in. Raya Software Limited, incorporated in Ireland (CRO). Not authorised or regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland.

## 3 · The full vision — five stages on one journey
0 Demo (today) → 1 Lending (possible under ECSPR) → 2 Rent share (possible under ECSPR) → 3 Tenant to owner (requires further authorisation) → 4 Internet of Value (depends on EU reform).

## 4 · Live: an investor funds a vacant home — Stage 1
Cork terrace, vacant since 2019. €160,000 secured loan, 8% fixed, 18 months, first legal charge, borrower pays the fee. Per €1,000 lent: €6.67 a month, €120.00 total interest, principal at term, lender fee €0. Journey: amount → onboarding (ID, AML, classification, 5-question test, loss simulation) → risk acknowledgement (+ €1,000/5% warning) → review (KIIS, 4-day reflection) → confirmation on Raya Chain.

## 5 · Live: the Raya Chain record — Stage 2
One master record. Rules travel with the asset (allowlist; reason-coded freeze and correction). Proofs travel, the record doesn’t (anchors to Chain B/C). Verify chain in the browser. A bank’s audit console, not a trading explorer.

## 6 · Live: what a supervisor could see — Stage 2
Raised per SPV vs the €5m ECSPR cap; classification; test pass rates; warnings; reflection withdrawals; AML alerts and freezes; complaints; audit hash-chain status; Raya Chain feed; CSV/JSON export. Illustrative supervisory view — not a Central Bank system.

## 7 · Rent share and monthly payouts — Stage 2
One company per home; notes; monthly net rent; vote on sale; ECSPR bulletin board. Fees 1.5% acquisition + 0.5%/yr administration. Dublin 8 per €1,000: net €985.00 → NOI share €53.09 → admin fee €4.93 → €48.16 a year (€4.01/month) before tax. 5.39% property yield → 4.82% investor yield.

## 8 · Tenant to owner — Stage 3
Tenant buys shares yearly; rent falls; investors bought out with a return; every step on Raya Chain. Illustration: Dublin 8, €18,600 a year from 5%. Requires further authorisation or a lending partner; affordability checks with human oversight.

## 9 · Internet of Value and the EU direction — Stage 4
Raya Chain as the official register; Layer Zero cross-chain holdings with compliance rules attached; payouts in a regulated euro stablecoin; settlement in central bank money. Depends on the reformed DLT Pilot Regime (Commission proposal, 4 Dec 2025).

## 10 · Roadmap — what we have and what we need
Have: Raya Software Ltd (CRO); Innovation Hub engagement; the MVP; the Raya Chain concept. Need (Stage 1): ECSP partner or own licence; payment institution; the first vacant home; a property manager. Stage 2: SPV structure, notes, legal sign-off on fund-rules risk. Stage 3: lending permissions or partner; affordability checks. Stage 4: DLT Pilot licence or partner; custodian; regulated euro stablecoin; cross-chain controls.

## 11 · Investor protections and ECSPR controls — Stage 1
Entry knowledge test; loss-bearing simulation; €1,000/5% specific warning; 4-day reflection; KIIS; €5m cap; verified-wallet allowlist; reason-coded freeze/correction; client money only with an authorised payment institution. Risk warning verbatim: “Property investments involve risk, including possible loss of capital. Returns are not guaranteed and investments may be illiquid.”
